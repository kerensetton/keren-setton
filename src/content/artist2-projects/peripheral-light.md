Peripheral Light began as a study in attention — specifically, what we fail to notice when we move through a city with purpose. Shot over eighteen months across Berlin, Hamburg, and Vienna, the series documents the transitional hour between late afternoon and night, when electric light and fading daylight compete for dominance and nothing is yet decided.

Each image in the series was made without artificial light, relying entirely on what was already present in the scene. The resulting photographs resist easy categorization: they are neither documentary nor fully abstract, but exist in the gap between the two. Figures appear as suggestions, architecture softens into geometry, and familiar streets become landscapes of pure sensation.

The work asks what it means to truly look — not to record, but to feel the weight of light as it moves.
