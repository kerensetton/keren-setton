Border Studies is a year-long photographic project made along the edges — literal and conceptual — of European geography. The series began at the former checkpoints of the Iron Curtain and expanded outward to include coastal zones, watershed divides, language boundaries, and the edges of urban sprawl where city becomes countryside without announcement.

Rather than documenting the dramatic or the political, the project looks for the ordinary: the gas station at the last exit before the border closes, the field where two agricultural systems meet along an invisible line, the apartment building whose address places it in one municipality but whose residents shop in another.

These are photographs of the places where categories fail. The world refuses to be divided as neatly as we require, and these images celebrate that refusal. Wherever a line has been drawn, life has found a way to blur it.
