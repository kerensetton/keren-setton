Frequency is a series of long-exposure photographs made exclusively in spaces designed around sound: concert halls emptied between performances, radio transmission towers, analog recording studios, and the acoustic chambers used for sound engineering research.

The images were made during periods of total silence — no recordings, no rehearsals, no signal. The camera captures these rooms in a state they were never designed for: the absence of the thing they exist to hold. The spaces seem almost to lean forward, to be waiting.

Technically, each exposure lasted between four and thirty minutes, made with the room's own lighting only. The results reveal architectural details invisible in normal use — the geometry of sound diffusion panels, the strange beauty of acoustic foam, the way a concert hall's ceiling is itself a kind of instrument.

Frequency asks whether a space shaped by one sense can teach us something about perception itself.
